## What the signals show now

Indonesia's anchor sector for this study is banking and finance. The evidence we can put on the
table runs from AI capability and adoption, through hiring, to the strain on workers — and its most
consistent feature is that the early-warning signals are visible while the labour-market outcome is
not.

**The work is exposed, and the exposed part is clerical.** ILO's occupational-exposure framework
puts 21.7% of Indonesian employment above minimal exposure to generative AI, and 93.9% of clerical
support workers — the group that includes bank back-office and customer-service roles — in some
exposed band. Indonesia's financial and insurance activities are the sector these workers sit in
(`[sector size 1.65m, Aug 2025 — unverified; confirm from BPS Keadaan Angkatan Kerja]`). Exposure is
not displacement: it is measured from task content, and the same ILO work notes transformation as
the more likely outcome than wholesale replacement.

**AI has already arrived — visibly, in named systems.** Five Indonesian banks describe AI in their
own disclosures. BCA names DIVA, a machine-learning collateral-valuation system, and VIRA, an NLP
customer-service system, as implemented. BRI dates its assistant Sabrina to 2018; by 2025 Sabrina
accounted for 45% of 21,535,701 interactions across all Contact BRI channels — a clerical task
demonstrably handled at scale. BNI reports limited analytical AI rather than decision-making, and
CIMB Niaga reports AI in internal audit. On the skills side, BCA reports 2,550 employees in
AI-related training during 2025. This is the S1–S2 evidence class: capability demonstrated and
employers naming the change — with the caveat that a channel-share figure is not a staffing figure,
and a training count is not a redeployment count.

**The diffusion context is population-level, not firm-level.** Microsoft's diffusion series puts
Indonesian generative-AI use at 11.7% of the 15–64 population in H1 2025, 12.7% in H2 2025 and
14.1% by Q1 2026. The source is vendor telemetry, so we grade it low-confidence and use it as
context only. Indonesia has no representative firm-level adoption series — that is a blind spot, not
a finding.

**Hiring is where the first real movement could appear, and the level tells us to be careful.**
Kemnaker's own portal counts finance and insurance postings falling from 1,195 (2022) to 797 (2023)
to 726 (2024), against a portal total that rose then fell. Our archived posting sample shows
entry-level openings at roughly one in ten of finance and BPO ads, half of them asking only for
upper-secondary education — the band in which clerical exposure is concentrated. Two cautions
govern this evidence. First, coverage: for 2024 our sample holds 66 finance-scoped rows against the
portal's own 726, about 9% — a share of a sample, not of the market. Second, explicitness: only 3 of
2,618 ads name an AI skill or tool, so ad text cannot carry the adoption signal on its own.

**Strain signals are the strongest series we have — and the hardest to read.** Indonesia's job-loss
insurance (JKP) paid 431,086 benefit payments to 90,513 workers in 2025, against 237,550 claim cases
in 2023 and 250,594 in 2024. A published service standard promises payment within three working
days; the achieved median is not published. In a May 2025 parliamentary hearing, 17% of JKP
applications were reported rejected for documentation problems. And PP 6/2025, effective 7 February
2025, changed benefit generosity, claim timing and contribution conditions — so 2024 and 2025 are
not directly comparable, and higher claims may reflect access rather than job loss.

**What is missing is the middle of the chain.** No Indonesian series links workplace AI adoption to
hires or wages; none records where dismissed finance workers go next; none reports hiring by
occupation inside banking. The bank-offices series exists and is published monthly, but has not yet
been extracted. That is the honest state of the evidence: capability and claims are observable, and
the hiring link in between is where Indonesia's monitoring is thinnest.

*Framing: every statement above is consistent with AI-driven change, not caused by it. Each row
keeps its alternative explanation — credit cycles, consolidation, employer recruiting practice and
regulation all move these numbers too.*
