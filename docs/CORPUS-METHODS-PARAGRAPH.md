# Corpus methods paragraph — for the paper

**28 Nov 2026** · to be reproduced wherever the postings evidence is used

> Job-advertisement evidence comes from an independent harvest of **1,035,176 advertisements** published on
> Indonesian and Philippine job boards between January 2024 and November 2026, of which **532,583 are
> Indonesian job cards and 152,641 carry a full job description**. Each advertisement was mapped from the
> platform's own category to an ISCO-08 occupation via a title-classification model, and each occupation was
> scored for AI task exposure on a 0-1 scale. Two-thirds of the corpus consists of job cards rather than full
> descriptions, so the skill-level analysis rests on the 152,641 Indonesian descriptions.
>
> The exposure estimates were validated in the only way available: a crawl-based estimate was compared with a
> same-month census of the same market, and the two agreed to within **0.57 of a percentage point**
> (0.4127 against 0.4144, Indonesia, September 2025). A second check compared two independent harvests of the
> same platform across 31 months; they agreed on the total (ratio 0.84, median monthly ratio 0.97) while
> differing by 0.30 to 1.67 in individual months. **We therefore treat the level as reliable and any
> single-month movement below 50% as harvest noise rather than signal.**
>
> Of the 256 Indonesian platform-months, **80 contain fewer than 100 advertisements** and are flagged rather
> than dropped; platform panels break in-year (one board shows 35,666 advertisements in 2025 and 8 in 2026).
> Aggregates are published for Indonesia only; the Philippine data is retained solely as a methodological
> comparison. No personal data is collected, no login or paywall is bypassed, and only aggregates - never raw
> advertisement text - enter the public repository.
>
> **The limitation that matters:** an advertisement measures what an employer published, not whether anyone
> was hired. Indonesian boards do not publish fill status. Every figure drawn from this corpus is therefore
> demand-side, and the hiring question this study exists to answer remains open.
