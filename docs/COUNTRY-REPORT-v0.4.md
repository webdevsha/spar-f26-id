# The empty chair
## Indonesia's AI shock will arrive as a hiring decision, not a dismissal

**SPAR Indonesia · country report and policy brief for AI councils · draft v0.4 · 28 November 2026**

---

## The finding

Indonesia's banks will not announce an AI shock. It will arrive as an empty chair: a teller who retires
and is not replaced, a school-leaver who applies and hears nothing, and beneath them a job-loss net that
reaches about one worker in four.

Three things are now measured rather than assumed. **Banking is the most AI-exposed sector in the
country's job market** — a mean exposure of 0.582, against 0.491 for sales and 0.452 for media — measured
across 20,088 Indonesian banking advertisements. **Banks are still hiring on net**: eight large banks added
8,490 more people than they lost last year. And **the one instrument built for job loss cannot see this
kind of shock**: to shift the national claims series by a detectable 1% would take 1.8 times the model's
entire five-year worst case, inside a single year.

So the risk is not that AI will dismiss Indonesian bank workers. It is that banks will stop hiring them,
quietly, and that no instrument we inventoried notices — because every protection starts at dismissal.

**What the councils can do now is small, specific and cheap:** point existing instruments at this shock
rather than invent new ones. Four asks, page 6.

## 1 · The ground: a large informal market and a small, heavy bank core

Employment rose to **147.99 million** in August 2026, from 131.05m in 2021. But formalisation has stopped:
the formal share reached **42.07%** in August 2024 and has not moved since. **57.93%** of workers are
informal — 85.73 million people — and 12.84m are underemployed. Open unemployment is **4.75%** (7.38m).

Finance and insurance is small in jobs and heavy in output: **1.62 million** workers, 1.09% of employment,
producing 4.11% of 2025 GDP, with bank assets at 57.7% of GDP. Average finance pay is **Rp5.35 million** a
month against a national Rp3.42m.

That combination is the whole problem in one line. Bank workers are formal, well paid, and therefore
**inside** the only meaningful protection Indonesia has. When a bank job disappears, the worker usually
leaves the protected 42% and lands in the informal 58%.

## 2 · Adoption: pilot scale

The only adoption-stage evidence for Indonesian banks reports **34% piloting, 18% running AI in some
areas, and 4% using it across functions with formal governance** — of 91 surveyed respondents, not of
banks. Governance maturity averages 2.74 of 5; 43 of 91 know the OJK AI guide.

Country-wide, **14.8%** of working-age Indonesians use AI (rank 76 of 147), against 18.8% globally. Use
rose 3.1 points in a year.

**Read it as a window, not a shield.** Low use means the arrival is paced by the largest banks — the ones
already piloting — and that tools will be imported, not built.

## 3 · What 1.03 million job ads show — and what they cannot

A harvest of **1,035,176 Indonesian and Philippine advertisements** across 35 months, scored occupation by
occupation, gives banking a measured exposure of **0.582** with **94.3%** of its ads in the highest
exposure groups (G2–G4). It also validates itself: on the one month with both a crawl and a census, the
weighted estimate lands within **0.57 of a percentage point** of the official figure (0.4127 vs 0.4144,
September 2025).

Two limits travel with it, and both are measured:

**Advertised demand is not hiring.** A posting says an employer wanted someone. It never says anyone was
hired. That is precisely the gap in signal S5.

**The monthly series is fragile.** **80 of 256** Indonesian platform-months carry fewer than 100 ads. And
where two independent harvests of the *same* platform can be compared — Dealls, 31 months — they agree on
the total (**ratio 0.84**, median month **0.97**) while differing by **0.30 to 1.67** in individual months.
Two harvests of the same evidence disagree by ±50% month to month. **No monthly claim smaller than that
is a finding.**

## 4 · The model: three speeds, one mechanism

Eight large banks — **218,129 staff**, December 2025, about 13% of finance and insurance employment. The
model applies exposure (0.582), then adoption, then a response order: **stop replacing leavers first, and
only dismiss if the cut exceeds attrition.** Attrition is 6.11% weighted, from each bank's reported leavers.

| | Slow | Medium | Extreme |
|---|---|---|---|
Tasks AI can take, by year | 30% (2030) | 52% (2029) | 85% (2028) |
Our probability prior | 40% | 50% | 10% |
Headcount 2030 vs 2026 | **−1.0%** | **−6.9%** | **−23.3%** |
Hiring cut | 5% | 32% | 62% |
Dismissals, 2026–2030 | **0** | **0** | **25,826** |
JKP cost | Rp0 | Rp0 | **Rp385.8 bn** |

**In the slow and medium cases nobody is dismissed.** The adjustment happens entirely through hiring: the
same frozen headcount is reached by not replacing leavers. That is what an empty chair looks like in a
model, and it is invisible to every dismissal-triggered instrument.

**The result is assumption-driven, and we say so.** Moving exposure from the old 30% placeholder to the
measured 0.582 multiplied the extreme case from 602 dismissals to 25,826. Changing the attrition basis
from all leavers to voluntary-only, at the old exposure, multiplied it from 602 to 7,718. Both runs are
published; neither is "the" answer.

## 5 · What the model cannot tell us: the backtest

We tested the model against the one series that claims to be a shock meter — national JKP claims.

Reported: **90,513** workers paid in 2025; 44,000+ to June 2026; about 64,000 to August 2026. Annualised,
those two mid-year points imply **88,000** and **96,000** — a swing of **8.8%** inside two months.

The model's extreme case implies roughly **100 claims a year** against that base: **0.11%**. **Signal to
noise, 1:80.** To move the annual series by a detectable 1%, the shock would have to produce **1,090
layoffs in one year — 1.8 times the model's entire five-year extreme total.**

Two verdicts, and they are different: **the model survives** (the reported path is flat within noise,
which is what slow and medium predict); **the instrument does not**. S8 is the strongest instrument in the
register and it is blind to this shock. Detection needs bank-level claims, not a national total.

## 6 · Who is protected, and what is ready to be pointed

Formal bank workers have the most cover. **JKP pays 60% of wage up to a Rp5m cap for six months** — a
maximum of Rp3.0m, just under 60% of an average finance wage. In 2025 **90,513 workers** were paid
**Rp1.01 tn**; **17%** of claims were rejected, mostly for documentation. **UU 27/2026**, in force since
28 October 2026, requires negotiation before layoffs and a severance reserve fund; its implementing rules
are due **28 October 2027**.

Everyone else is thinner. Informal workers (85.73m) get no JKP. Entry-level applicants are covered by
nothing, because nobody has dismissed them.

Of **24 inventoried instruments**, 17 clearly reach bank workers, only 2 clearly reach informal workers or
entrants, and **4 state how they are funded**. The AI-specific ones — the OJK AI guide, the national AI
strategy, the AI Perpres drafts — carry **no labour clause and no monitoring**. The worker-support ones —
JKP, PP 6/2025, Permenaker 2/2025, Prakerja, Voc50k — are not tied to AI.

**That is the useful news: the delivery routes exist.** The ask is to point them, not to build.

## 7 · Where the evidence is not

Five gaps matter most: (1) same-function hiring after an AI rollout; (2) the link between branch closures
and staff; (3) JKP training outcomes and processing time; (4) where workers go after they leave; (5) any
link between a bank's AI adoption and its labour outcomes. **No official outlook from OJK or PERBANAS
names AI** — unlike the Philippines, where the industry body revised its outlook.

The register now records gaps honestly: 48 evidence rows, of which 6 are contradictions carried side by
side rather than resolved (a 9.1 million interaction figure absent from both BRI PDFs; an office-count
series that rises 2,503 in a series that otherwise falls; a firm-adoption rate of 14.9% against "about 3%"
in the same release). **Contradictions published are worth more than a smoothed series.**

The six readiness questions are now named — reach, who is missed, trigger and timing, funding, delivery
latency, and monitoring — but **not yet scored**, pending the team's confirmation.

## 9 · How to read this

**A stress test, not a forecast.** Scenario probabilities, task exposure and the hiring response are
judgement placeholders, set against Moody's Analytics scenario design and adjusted for Indonesia. Every
number above carries its source in the workbook and the atlas; every assumption is labelled as one.

Three caveats worth stating plainly. The 0.582 exposure score is a **task-based measure transferred by
title**, not measured displacement. The eight-bank panel is **13% of the sector**, not the sector. And the
postings evidence measures **advertised demand to within ±50% a month**, which is not the same thing as
hiring.
