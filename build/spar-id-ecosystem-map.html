SPAR Fall 2026 · Indonesia lane · ecosystem & policy mapping (v1)

Three maps, not one

The ecosystem map shows who is positioned where. The policy map shows what the rules and programmes actually say. The bridge maps the join — and that join is where this lane earns its place: the institution best able to observe a problem is usually not the institution authorised to act on it. Kept separate so that claim is visible instead of blurred. Indonesia only; the Philippines lane is the contrast case.

Framing

Organised around functions, not a flat list of organisations — who observes AI deployment, who observes workers, who delivers support, and who can authorise a response. No single stakeholder can establish an AI-related labour shock: banks document deployment, labour institutions observe separations and support uptake, BPS shows population outcomes, and financial and fiscal authorities assess wider transmission. These maps connect those partial views without assuming they are already linked.

Ecosystem boundary — Three nested populations

  • Core financial-sector workforce — Employees of banks, insurers, financing and securities firms - aligns with industry employment statistics.
  • Finance-serving workforce — Outsourced customer service, collections, document processing, IT, cybersecurity, recruitment contractors - work changes with a bank's AI deployment while employment sits outside the finance industry.
  • Affected households & local economies — Displaced workers, dependants, new entrants, informal destinations, concentrated finance-employment communities. Needed for M4 and M6.

First case: Banking as anchor, outsourced functions tracked explicitly; insurance and fintech as comparative subcases, not mixed into one sample.

The six measures

  • M1 Sector employment and exposure — Where is potentially affected work concentrated?
  • M2 AI deployment and workforce change — What has actually been deployed, and how has work changed?
  • M3 Recruitment and vacancies — Is access to jobs, or the content of recruitment, changing?
  • M4 Job loss and worker destinations — Who exits, and where do they go?
  • M5 Worker support capacity — Can support reach affected workers and restore employment or earnings?
  • M6 Consumption and fiscal effects — Is adjustment spreading into households, public finances or social-insurance financing?

Institutional responsibilities are source-backed. Proposed data requests, pain points and research roles are analytical recommendations — not claims that any entity already collects or will release the fields. Anchor sector: Finance & insurance (banking), clerical support.

Policy & escalationwho can authorise

OJK - financial regulator & supervisor

Public bank statistics and disclosures; request aggregated AI-use/function information and workforce-transition reporting

The stronger first door for bank-level deployment context. Prudential indicators can look healthy while employment pathways worsen; supervisory data is confidential; separating digitalisation from LLM-specific change is hard. The RP3I roadmap already addresses digital transformation and governance.

M2M1M3M4

spar26-ecosystem.md s2, s5

Bank Indonesia - central bank

Payment-system activity, financial conditions, macro context - aggregate indicators, not customer records

Supplies macro and payment controls. Payment growth is not a measure of labour welfare; its boundaries differ from OJK's integrated supervision; aggregate indicators dilute local stress.

Public APBN KiTa revenue/spending; aggregated labour-related revenue and programme allocation via DJP and DJPb

M6 primarily, plus M5 programme financing. National tax changes are highly confounded; taxpayer confidentiality; bank profitability can rise while payroll falls. APBN KiTa gives budget execution, not AI-attributed fiscal loss.

Policy map

22 nodes · 8 edges

Documents, monitoring and levers on their own - what exists, what it observes, and what it can do.

Kept separate from the ecosystem map on purpose. The ecosystem map shows who is positioned where; this shows what the rules and programmes actually say. Two questions per document: does it name AI-driven job loss at all, and does it monitor anything? On the current three-document inventory the answer to both is largely no, and that is the finding - a policy layer with real instruments (JKP, Karirhub) that were not built to watch this transition.

Statutory & supervisorythe rules that bind

OJK AI Guide - Panduan AI untuk Perbankan

Guideline. AI governance guidance for banks.

Named AI job loss as a risk: NO - it governs AI use, not labour outcomes. Owner and budget not stated in source. Monitoring not stated. No action rule. This is the q1-q6 grade, and the gap is the point.

14 nodes · 14 edges

The join: for each measure, who observes it, who can validate it, who is authorised to act, and who delivers.

This is the view that earns its own map. Name a problem's best observer and you have not named anyone empowered to fix it - on this map that is true for M1, M3, M4 and M6. The four labels are deliberately separate so the diagram cannot imply that the institution best able to observe a problem is also the institution authorised or equipped to solve it.
