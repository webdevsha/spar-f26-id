Overview

Two collection arms make this dataset. Every row is public: the Kemnaker Karirhub portal today, and Internet Archive captures of the same public postings for earlier years. The composition of the two arms is different — live postings publish far richer text — so any series that mixes them must be read as a level, not a trend. That single fact governs everything below.

Highlights

Seven findings that survive their own caveats. Each one is recomputable from the CSVs; none of them is a hiring number.

1

The AI skill signal is essentially absent from Indonesian finance and BPO ad text

Only 3 of 2,618 postings name an AI skill or tool in their requirements — 0.1%. Data skills appear in 175 rows (6.7%), everyday digital literacy in 966 (36.9%).

Evidence the ai_tier, ai_evidence and ai_source columns — each flagged row carries the matched term and its sentence.

Caveat Ad text measures what employers choose to advertise, not what they deploy. S1/S2 evidence has to come from bank disclosures; this dataset cannot carry it alone.

2

Entry-level hiring runs near one in ten — and the 2026 “rise” is an artefact

Entry-level wording appears in 103 of 983 2024 rows (10.5%), 125 of 1,347 in 2025 (9.3%), and 50 of 288 in 2026 (17.4%).

Evidence the per-year counts above, split by arm in the overview panel.

Caveat 2026 is 97% live-portal rows with full requirement text, earlier years are archive rows with thinner text — the 2026 figure is a text-coverage effect, not a hiring recovery. Do not plot it as a trend.

3

Half the advertised work sits at the education band that exposure research flags

1,273 rows (48.6%) ask only for SMA/SMK — upper secondary. Bachelor (S1) is 706 rows (27.0%).

Evidence the min_education column, bucketed in the builder.

Caveat Education is what the employer states; the archive arm states it less often. Treat as a distribution across the dataset, not a year-on-year comparison.

4

Advertised demand is concentrated, and one step removed from the banks

The single largest employer in the dataset accounts for 287 rows (11.0%) — an outsourcing/payroll intermediary, not a bank. Sales and marketing is the largest function (892 rows).

Evidence top employers: PT Infomedia Solusi Humanika (287), PT Swakarya Insan Mandiri (57), PT. Multipratama Indahraya (43), PT Infomedia Solusi Humanika Jakarta (39); top functions: Penjualan dan Pemasaran (892), Manufaktur dan Produksi (278), Akuntansi & Keuangan (84), Kredit / Pinjaman (77).

Caveat Many in-scope rows are therefore indirect — a worker hired by a staffing firm serving a bank. Sector attribution in ad text is weak; 80% of rows are sector-tagged by heuristic, not by the employer.

5

Two provinces carry two fifths of it

DKI Jakarta (553) and Jawa Tengah (521) together hold 41.0% of rows; Jawa Barat follows at 379.

Evidence DKI Jakarta 553, Jawa Tengah 521, Jawa Barat 379, Banten 181, Jawa Timur 158, Sulawesi Tengah 83.

Caveat Geography is where the employer is, and archive rows lose location text more often than live rows. Use it to target interviews, not to size regional shocks.

6

Pay is not readable from this source

1,121 rows keep the salary hidden (42.8%); only 278 state an explicit “yes” and a minority print a range.

Evidence the salary_shown column.

Caveat Wage movement — the M1 and M6 question — must come from BPS Sakernas and bank annual reports. This dataset can never answer it.

7

The wall is in front of the question, not behind it

80.4% of rows (2,106) sit outside finance scope and were kept rather than deleted — as a measurement of how hard sector attribution is.

Evidence the in_scope and sector columns.

Caveat A larger scrape with cleaner employer metadata is what turns this from a pilot into a series. That is the next collection decision, and it is a data-quality decision before it is a scale decision.

Policy scenarios

Four futures to think with — not forecasts. Each names the signal it moves, the body that would have to act, the policy question it raises, and the observation that would show it is wrong. They are written for a decision-maker reading on a screen, not a ranking of governments.

A

Gradual augmentation

AI is inserted inside existing workflows. Tasks shrink, roles stay. Hiring thins at the entry level first, because that is where task bundles are most codifiable — while headcount, wages and unemployment barely move.

Signal watched
S3 → S5, with S1/S2 as the upstream condition
What moves first
The entry-level share of finance and BPO vacancies, quarter on quarter.
Who acts
The banks themselves; OJK through its AI governance guide for banking; Kemnaker through vacancy data.
The policy question
If entry-level vacancies thin while total employment holds, what is the first instrument that reaches a fresh graduate who never gets hired at all? Income support only triggers on losing a job.
What would falsify it
Entry-level share stable or rising through two consecutive quarters while AI uptake in bank disclosures increases.
B

Uneven disruption

Automation lands bank by bank and province by province according to branch economics and back-office footprint, not nationally. Clerical reductions cluster; branch and office counts start to fall in specific provinces while others are untouched.

Signal watched
S6 + S7, with S8 confirming
What moves first
Bank office and branch counts by province; layoff notifications filed with provincial manpower offices.
Who acts
Provincial manpower offices; PERBANAS as the industry body; OJK as supervisor; local training providers.
The policy question
If the shock is geographic, does readiness have to be geographic too? A national programme cannot reach one province's clerical cohort faster than a provincial one can.
What would falsify it
Net branch counts flat or rising, and no clustering of verified reductions in any province over two quarters.
C

Rapid substitution

Contact-centre and back-office tasks go end-to-end. The clearest publicly visible case already exists: an Indonesian bank's assistant handling nearly half of its contact interactions. Volume of job-loss claims becomes the first hard evidence — and the buffer becomes the story.

Signal watched
S8 + S9, with M5 as the capacity test
What moves first
JKP claim volume and the share of applications rejected or delayed; PEKA and training take-up.
Who acts
BPJS Ketenagakerjaan (JKP); Kemnaker (training, placement); Ministry of Finance if the fiscal line is hit.
The policy question
Does help reach the worker? A claim system with documentation friction and no published processing time converts a shock into an unmeasured one — the failure mode is administrative, not economic.
What would falsify it
JKP claim volume flat and application-rejection share falling — that combination would mean either no shock or a claim system that is absorbing it successfully.
D

The data-blind case

The shock arrives and nobody can see it. No public series links workplace AI adoption to hires, wages, output or tax bases, and no series records where dismissed finance workers go next. Policy responds to anecdote — or does not respond.

Signal watched
the five stated gaps in the signal register
What moves first
Whether the five gaps in the register stay gaps: worker destinations · adoption→hires link · claim latency · function-level hiring · training outcomes.
Who acts
BPS and Kemnaker (collection); the researchers who can request the microdata (LPEM UI, SMERU); international partners (ILO, World Bank, ESCAP) who fund the survey module.
The policy question
Is the intervention a policy, or a measurement instrument? A single linked employer–employee series would make scenarios A, B and C testable instead of arguable.
What would falsify it
Any one of the five gaps closes — that immediately downgrades this scenario.

Read together: A and B are separable by province — B is A plus geography. C and D are the two outcomes of the same stress test: does the evidence system see the shock, and does the support system reach the worker? The scenarios are ordered by what would have to be true for the policy response to still be timely, not by likelihood.

Where this sits — and what the archive-scale study changes

A companion study (Job-posting for the Philippines and Indonesia: structure and AI exposure, 23 November 2026) works at a different scale from the same public sources: 1,059,854 postings, 13 platforms, January 2024 → November 2026, all of it read out of two public web archives (Common Crawl and the Wayback Machine) rather than from the platforms. It is the scale our collection should grow into, and it publishes the method to copy.

Three kinds of record — never added together

the study's own split, for comparison

Kind A · platform counts (open ads by category and month)0
Kind B · job cards (title, employer, date, teaser — no description)827,286
Kind C · full posting pages (description, experience, skills)232,568

Kind B: Philippines 310,266 · Indonesia 517,020. Kind C: Philippines 48,058 · Indonesia 184,510, drawn from 13 platforms. Our dataset is one arm of Kind C plus nothing else — which is why our shares can move when the archive's text coverage moves.

The method fix we should copy

what it corrects in our own numbers

  • Weight to platform totals. Read the platform's own count of open ads per category and month (their Kind A), then weight our rows to it. That is the correct answer to the artefact in highlight 2 — our 2026 rows look richer only because they are live rows.
  • Only compare inside one platform, on months both years have, among postings captured within 90 days of posting, with a range on every figure.
  • Audit the AI measure. Exclude boilerplate repeated under three or more titles, the employer's own hiring-process notices, employer self-description, and “AI” meaning Adobe Illustrator; full descriptions only; hand-check a sample to bound it. Our 3 rows become a defensible upper bound rather than a bare count.
  • Report the denominator. Entry level is a share of descriptions that state an experience requirement — a description silent on experience is excluded, never treated as requiring experience.

The coverage check this makes possible. For 2024 — the one year where both exist — this dataset holds 66 finance/BPO-scoped rows against the portal's own 726 finance postings for that year: about 9.1%. That is the number to quote whenever anyone reads a share off this dataset as if it described the market. It describes a sample of it, and now we can say how large a sample.
Caveat on quota: “Kuota Lowongan” means advertised worker slots, and it swings far more than postings do (46,556 slots on 1,195 postings in 2022, 7,334 on 726 in 2024). Quota plausibly counts repeat or multi-slot ads without deduplication. Use postings for trend, never quota.

Read this before quoting any posting share: job ads cover professional and associate-professional work densely and manual work far less than employment does — in the study's own comparison, ISCO groups 6–9 are 11.9% of ads against 55.2% of wage employees. So a posting-based share describes advertised demand, never the labour market.

Limits, stated on the record

This is advertised demand, not hiring. A posting can be reposted, never filled, or filled by another route. It is a leading indicator by design — hiring moves before headcount — and it is only usable with that framing attached.

The two arms are not interchangeable. 2,337 archive rows and 281 live rows differ in text richness and field coverage. Mixing them is acceptable for a level, not for a trend.

Five things nobody collects yet: where dismissed workers go next · any link from firm AI adoption to hires or wages · JKP approval and payment latency · hiring by occupation inside banking · placement and earnings outcomes of JKP training. These are named as gaps rather than proxied.

No personal data. Aggregates and shares only, never raw individual ads, in anything shared beyond the team.
